# World-screen UI

Selected October 10, 2026: modern controls with the existing pixel-art map. The October 11 polish pass extends this treatment to map annotations and new-game setup; city management, research, diplomacy and other screens retain their original styling.

- Charcoal surfaces, off-white text and a restrained teal interaction accent. Civilization portraits retain the game's ownership colors.
- A compact top bar with civilization identity, economy, turn/year and strategic resources. In agent spectator matches it follows the active civilization. Stat clicks inspect that same civilization; Overview opens the live all-civilization panel. These controls do not switch the map's viewing perspective or grant turn authority.
- Narrow windows stack identity and statistics instead of overlapping controls. Long identity labels truncate; full civilization names remain in the activity feed.
- Smaller Overview, Fog of War, Pause/Resume, Activity, next-turn and auxiliary controls. Map-layer toggles use neutral square controls and a selected tint.
- A floating activity feed with configured agent names, stable agent colors, civilization portraits and turn separators. Messages are brighter and provider reasoning is subdued. Consecutive tool calls collapse into the latest call and an expandable count; messages, errors and agent/turn changes end each group. Expand a group to see every call, then a call to inspect its real arguments/results and Copy details.
- Every message and thinking entry repeats the colored agent name; tool groups and errors identify their speaker too. All floating panels use a translucent charcoal surface and subtle rim drawn from existing skin textures. They no longer capture or blur the map, so opening panels creates no additional framebuffer or shader resources.
- Live follows the latest entry immediately as streaming text and tool results grow. Mouse-wheel steps are 24 pixels. Scrolling or expanding a tool suspends following, preserves the entry being read even as earlier paragraphs grow, and counts new entries. Live jumps to the latest entry and resumes following; Earlier loads older activity; Logs opens the saved journal directory.
- The feed follows the active nation by default. All and fixed-nation filters are available to spectators; human viewers retain their existing activity visibility limits. Reading earlier activity holds the current nation and position until Live is selected.

Colors and button states live in the scoped `GameUiStyle.kt`, with `WorldScreenStyle.kt` retaining its compatible facade. The global skin is unchanged. The original menu shortcuts, map pan/zoom, agent pause boundaries and gameplay authority checks are retained. The feed is an ordinary scene actor; only its rectangle captures input.

The Overview button (or existing Empire Overview shortcut) toggles a floating, draggable Civilizations panel, initially on the right. This is an extension of the established HUD, with the same translucent surface, type hierarchy and stable agent accents. The map and match controls remain active outside its rectangle. A sortable comparison sits above selected-nation details; narrow layouts retain score/science and move the remaining stats into details where permitted. [Civilization overview](civilization-overview.md) records its filters, interaction and privacy behavior.

The panel reads the GL-thread game publication, refreshes twice per second and on world updates, and preserves selection, search, sorting and available scroll positions across snapshots. It clamps its position and layout when resized and disposes its timer with the world screen. Spectators see every nation, including defeated nations, and full statistics. Human viewers see themselves and met nations; existing ranking visibility settings apply to public comparisons. Private opponent economy, research queues, resource inventory, city production and unit lists remain hidden even when rankings are allowed. Opening the panel never changes the selected map civilization or gameplay authority.

City plaques use neutral surfaces and distinct population, defence and aircraft badges. Your own cities keep a heavy top rim, and cities at war with you show a heavy rim plus the war icon, so these states survive zooming out and do not depend on hue alone. Nation identity uses the nation's own inner/outer colour pair as an icon disc, so city-states and dark palettes stay legible. Long names wrap to at most two lines and end with "..."; the city screen keeps the full name. Their visibility and interaction predicates are unchanged. The minimap uses a quiet frame, contrasting camera outlines and labeled existing layer controls. Its resize control draws only a compact plate behind the icon, with a larger transparent hit area. Wrap projection and map navigation remain unchanged.

Setup chooses stacked options below 1080 logical pixels or in portrait windows; the stacked column is capped at 720 pixels and centred. Nation cards separate identity from actions and wrap model and bonus summaries. The control button names its setting ("Control: Human"), and removing a slot that has an agent profile asks for confirmation. Editor Save/Cancel controls stay outside the scrollable fields, and the visible Start action uses the original validation/loading state. The footer places Back on the left, then Reset, Presets and a wider primary Start. When Start is disabled, the footer says what is missing. Start has no Enter shortcut, so a key pressed in a seed or size field cannot begin a paid agent match.

Deferred: user resizing of individual panels, feed-to-map links, historical charts/replay, replacement terrain art and the remaining application screens.
